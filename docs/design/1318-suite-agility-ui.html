Encryption suites in the browser: the suite choice, the rotate-then-confirm flow, the suite on every copy and the health tile

Issue #1318 (T2.3), dated 2026-11-10. Parent epic #1310, FR-89 and FR-84. I drew this before the component work and ahead of T1.5 (#1315), T2.1 (#1316) and T2.2 (#1317), against the shapes those issues fix: GET /encryption/suites with the Describe() sentence and counts, the policy's suite and rotation_needed, key rotate --suite, the deprecated and retired health sentences, and the re-encrypt operation with its dry run. Section 9 lists what it leans on. Every surface is drawn four times: light, dark, and narrow in each, in the token set from ui/shared/src/design-system/tokens.css, and in the vocabulary 994-retained-copy-encryption.html settled (the Encrypted chip and the Verified recoverable chip stay two chips; nothing here changes either beyond adding the suite id).

Status: a mock-up, superseded where the product differs.

A picture drawn on 2026-11-10 to settle decisions for the component work in the same issue. Where the product and this note disagree, the product wins. The Describe() sentences drawn here are placeholders of the right shape: the build prints the registry's own sentence and never one typed in the UI. No key, recipient, canary or credential appears anywhere in this file or its render. A post-quantum recipient is about 2000 characters, so wherever one could ever be drawn it is clipped at 80 with a copy action (section 6), and none is drawn in full here.

1. Settings: the Suite choice inside the Retained-copy encryption card

Decision. The card gains one group, Suite, listing every suite the registry has, in registry order, each with its id in mono, a status badge and the suite's own Describe() sentence under it. The default carries a Default badge (ok edge and fill), a supported suite Supported (neutral edge), a deprecated suite Deprecated, retiring in V (warn edge and fill), and a retired suite is shown, dashed and disabled, with Retired and the reason it cannot be chosen. The current choice is the policy's resolved suite; when the configuration says default the line under the group says so, because "follows the build's default" and "pinned to age-pq/v1" are different promises across an upgrade.

Why a radio group and not a native select. A native <option> cannot carry a sentence under it, a badge beside it or a reason next to a disabled entry, and its disabled ink is the browser's, which cannot be measured or held to 4.5:1. The issue asks for the status beside each option and the reason beside a retired one, so every option is a row that can show them, measured in section 8. It is still one choice with one value, and the keyboard moves through it as a radio group does.

Off is unchanged. With encryption off the card stays one switch and one sentence, as #994 decided: no Suite group is drawn, because there is nothing to write under a suite until encryption is on. Enabling creates the first key under the policy's suite, which reads as the default unless a configuration pins one.

2. Changing the suite: patch, then Rotate key, Download bundle, Confirm, in that order

Decision. Choosing a suite opens one dialog. Its first step states the effect from the code (new copies go under the new suite once it has an active key, every existing copy keeps its recorded suite and key and stays readable, nothing is re-encrypted) and echoes retnd encryption policy patch --suite <id>. Confirming patches the policy and re-reads it. When the answer says rotation_needed, the same dialog moves to Rotate key (echo retnd encryption key rotate --suite <id>), then to Download bundle (the existing one-time export, its acknowledgement and its step-up), then to Confirm (the existing statement, never an upload). A strip of three steps at the top shows where the operator is and what is done.

Leaving early says what is owed. Cancel, Escape or a click outside on any step after the patch does not close the dialog straight away: it replaces the body with "Still owed" and the list of what is left (an active key for the suite, a downloaded bundle, a confirmation), each with the command that does it, and two buttons, Back to the steps and Leave, I will finish later. Leaving keeps the policy patched, because it was written, and the card and the Dashboard keep the degraded states that already exist: "No active key for suite X" (danger, copies under the policy are refused before anything is deleted) until the rotation, and "No confirmed recovery bundle" until the confirmation.

Nothing about the key is held. The rotation answer is mapped to an id, a status, a suite and timestamps; a recipient or any other long string in it is dropped at the client, and the bundle step is the same download that holds the bytes only in the client function's locals.

3. The suite on every encrypted copy, on Backup detail and in the Backups list

Decision. The Encrypted chip gains the suite id after its word, in mono and in --text-2, inside the same chip: Encrypted age-pq/v1. The second chip (Verified recoverable, Not decrypt-verified, Key missing, Decrypt failed) is unchanged. An age/v1 and an age-pq/v1 copy on one page read as two different ids in two chips, which is the whole point. A copy whose record names no suite (an older server) draws the chip as before with no id rather than a guessed one. The stored-bytes digest line on Backup detail names the suite in place of the bare format: "(suite age-pq/v1 v1, key mk-...)".

4. Health: deprecated and retired suite counts on the Dashboard, and Re-encrypt under current suite with its dry run first

Decision. The Dashboard's encryption rows (the key-custody tile) gain one row per suite that is deprecated or retired and still recorded by an active copy or a key, in T2.1's words, exactly as suiteVerdict writes them: "12 active copies are under age/v1, a deprecated suite; retirement announced for version 0.9.0", or "3 active copies record mock-retired/v1, retired in 0.8.0; they still read, and re-encrypting them under the active suite clears this". Each row has Re-encrypt under current suite, which opens the T2.2 operation with the dry run already requested: the dialog lists the copies by key with their destination and every per-copy refusal (same medium only, key unavailable, already current) in the engine's words, and the Re-encrypt button is armed only once that dry run has answered. Its echo is retnd encryption reencrypt --suite <id> --dry-run before and the same line without --dry-run on the button.

Zero is silence. A deployment with every copy under supported suites draws none of this, as a deployment that encrypts nothing draws nothing today.

5. Command echoes: the four verbs, from the one function the terminal uses

retnd encryption suites under the Suite group, retnd encryption policy patch --suite <id> on the change step, retnd encryption key rotate --suite <id> on the rotate step and retnd encryption reencrypt --suite <id> [--dry-run] in the re-encrypt dialog. All four come from encryptionCommands.ts, so the line beside a control and the terminal's echo of the request cannot disagree, and the terminal never says a suite action has no equivalent.

6. Long strings: nothing over 80 characters is drawn whole

No surface in this issue draws a recipient, and the client's mapping drops one if a response carries it. As a second line of defence, every runtime string the new surfaces print (an id, a refusal sentence quoted from the engine) goes through one clip: up to 80 characters it is drawn as it is, past that it is cut at 80 with an ellipsis and a Copy button that copies the whole value to the clipboard without putting it in the page. The canary rows plant a 2000-character post-quantum-shaped recipient in the mock's responses for the Settings card and the backup page and assert the page never holds it.

The first draft filled the selected option with --accent-quiet. A neutral badge edge (--edge-3) on that fill measures 2.90:1 in light, under the 3:1 a control edge needs, so the selected row keeps the plain surface and is marked by a 2px --accent edge (5.51:1 and 6.36:1) and by its checked radio.

The disabled option is not faded with opacity, which is how a disabled control usually loses its contrast: it keeps --text-2, a dashed edge and the word Retired, so it reads as unavailable by its shape and its words rather than by being hard to read.

9. What this note leans on

Written against the issue bodies on 2026-11-10 and checked against the merged contract the same day (T1.5 #1325, T2.1 #1327, T2.2 #1326). GET /encryption/suites answers suites[] with id, version, status, default, describe (the Describe() sentence), retirement_version (announced for a deprecated suite, or the one that retired it) and the active_copies and keys counts. The policy carries suite and suite_configured, and a patch with suite answers rotation_needed and rotate_command without rotating. A key carries suite, and POST /encryption/keys/rotate?suite= rotates into one. A placement carries encryption_suite next to encryption_key_id (added in this issue: the copy record's format_id is the suite id). The re-encrypt dry run is GET /encryption/reencrypt with suite, key or deprecated and an optional destination, answering groups by suite and key with a verdict, refusal code and reason per copy; the run is POST with destination_medium required, so the dialog takes the destination the dry run proposed and echoes it with --to. The health section's sentence is not on the wire, so the tile composes it from these counts in the same words.
