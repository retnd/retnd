The 16-pixel favicon: wordmark, d monogram, or the mark

Issue #893 (EPIC R, FR-44) · 2026-09-14 · the decision, and the pixels it was taken on.

The question FR-44 asks

The lockup is a mark plus a four-glyph wordmark with the daemon accent on the trailing d. Every size above 32 px can carry both. 16 px cannot carry the wordmark, so 16 px is the size that forces a choice, and FR-44 requires the choice to be recorded here rather than left as whatever the export script happened to produce. Three candidates were rendered at exactly 16 px by rsvg-convert and are shown below at 1:1 and at 8× with nearest-neighbour scaling, so what is on the page is the pixel grid a browser tab actually gets and not a smooth re-drawing of it.

A — the mark

Reads. The broken ring and the centre dot stay separable: the counter survives as a ring of background pixels and the gap in the stroke is still visible at the lower left. It is also the only candidate that is the same drawing as the 32 px, 48 px, 180 px and 256 px exports.

B — the wordmark

Does not read, and it is not close. Fitting the wordmark to 16 px of width puts each glyph on about 2.7 px and the stroke on 0.39 px, so every stem is a partial-coverage grey and every counter is gone. Four glyphs become one smudge. This candidate is here to be ruled out on the pixels rather than on assertion.

C — a d monogram

Reads — and that is the problem. Drawn purpose-wide (bowl 13 units against the lockup d’s 8) the counter survives and it is legibly a d. But see the decision below: legible is not the bar a favicon is held to.

Decision

A — the mark, unchanged

At 16 px the favicon is the mark. It is neither the wordmark nor a d monogram, and no monogram is drawn. Three reasons, in the order they decided it:

  • A monogram is legible and not identifying. A favicon is judged in a tab strip against twenty other favicons, where the question is not “can this be read” but “can this be told apart”. A lowercase d in a round monoline is the least distinctive glyph this name could offer; the broken ring is a shape, and a shape is what survives being 16 px wide in peripheral vision.
  • The accent cannot survive a monogram at this size. The one thing FR-44 insists the art keeps is the daemon accent on the trailing d, and the accent is a tonal split between the d and the letters before it. A monogram has no letters before it, so the split has nothing to be a split from; painting the lone d in the muted tone would render a washed-out favicon and painting it in the full tone would drop the accent silently. Candidate C therefore cannot carry the one property the redraw is required to preserve.
  • It keeps one drawing across every declared size. Choosing a monogram at 16 px makes 16 px the only size whose favicon is a different object from its own 32 px sibling, which is the kind of divergence that outlives the person who introduced it — and this repository already selected the mark once, as Option 1a “Cycle” (Logo Options.dc.html), on the explicit ground that it holds up at 16 px in a NAS tab bar.

What this makes true elsewhere: favicon-16.png, favicon-32.png, the three favicon.ico members, apple-touch-icon.png and both logo-mark-*.png exports are all the mark and only the mark. The wordmark appears only in the two lockup files, docs/assets/logo-light.svg and logo-dark.svg.

Where the wordmark stops working, measured

160px lockup width · x-height 14.9px · stroke 3.93px
122px lockup width · x-height 11.4px · stroke 3.00px
96px lockup width · x-height 9.0px · stroke 2.36px
72px lockup width · x-height 6.7px · stroke 1.77px
56px lockup width · x-height 5.2px · stroke 1.37px

The floor is about 72 px of lockup width — an x-height of 6.7 px and a stroke of 1.77 px, where every counter is still an open pixel. At 56 px (stroke 1.37 px) it is soft but still reads; below that the e closes up first. README.md embeds the lockup at 175 px, which is the width that keeps the mark rendering at exactly the size it did before the re-fit.

What this note does not decide

Wordmark, monogram or mark at 16 pxDecided here, on the renders above.
Whether the letterforms themselves are good Not decided. The glyphs are a geometric construction — monoline stroke 3 on the mark’s own 48-unit grid, one glyph width, one x-height, one ascender, arcs that are exact semicircles and half-ellipses — fitted so the four glyphs read. That is a constructed wordmark, not a drawn one: no optical stem correction, no overshoot on the round glyphs, no ink traps, and no designer has looked at it. FR-44’s human acceptance step is where that judgement belongs, and docs/design/brand-assets.md records it as outstanding.
Whether the accent tone is the right tone Not decided. #8b8d8a on light and #767976 on dark are carried over unchanged from the previous lockup, deliberately, so the re-fit changes one variable.
